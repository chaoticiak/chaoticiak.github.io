About the campaign

Togetrick is presented as a campaign, divided into numerous worlds. Most worlds are regular worlds, which follows some general rules as explaind below. Regular worlds are typically collections of tasks, usually related in theme, and sometimes with a world-wide mechanic. However, special worlds defy categorization; each one is a new surprise. You can read the list of worlds. At the beginning of the campaign, you only have access to one world, Startia.

Most of the content in a world are initially inaccessible, locked behind envelopes represented as white boxes. Do not open these until instructed! Once you are allowed to open an envelope, click/tap it and read its text. Envelopes typically add new tasks to a regular world, but some envelopes introduce major mechanics that change the world quite drastically.

Each world has one or more objectives. Each objective is given in a blue box. When you complete the objective, click/tap the box to reveal a reward, which typically lets you open an envelope or gives you access to new worlds. The campaign has meta-progression, so sometimes a reward lets you open an envelope in another world. Therefore, some advanced objectives in a world may require you to explore other worlds first.

Worlds vary wildly in size. Some worlds are small enough that they can be fully completed in a single game session. Others may require multiple hours to go through. The entire campaign will undoubtedly take a while to play, so you are recommended to keep a record your campaign so you can pause playing and pick it up another time.

Togetrick is presented as a campaign, but I always think of the campaign to be a loose thread to connect a variety of different ways to play using the same basic rules.

If you don't like the campaign, don't let it restrain you. If you don't like the unlock system, feel free to open everything. If you have your own ideas inspired by this campaign, go ahead and play them. In fact, feel free to send me your suggestions; I love to see people iterating on a design, and maybe your ideas might end up being part of the campaign.

Regular world

A regular world is a collection of tasks. Each task only cares about a single deal. This means, when playing in a regular world, each deal is independent; you don't have to record progress inside a task.

There are two kinds of tasks: binary tasks and score tasks.

Binary tasks are yes/no. You either succeed or fail, there is no in-between.

Score tasks award some number of points depending on your performance on the deal. Some score tasks have fail conditions; meeting the fail condition means you fail the task and get no score.

In general, your records — which tasks you have succeeded, what your high scores are — will not be reset. Once you record a success on a binary task, it won't later become failed. Once you record a certain high score, that number won't later be reduced.

Some regular worlds have additional rules applicable only for that world. If they conflict with these general rules, the specific world-wide rules take precedence.

Playing in a regular world

Before distributing cards: If you play in a regular world, first choose one or more tasks to attempt. Usually you choose only one task, but it's legal to choose more.

In order for a deal to be successful, you must succeed on all chosen tasks. (A binary task either succeeds or fails. A score task typically automatically succeeds, but it may fail if there is a fail condition.) If any of the tasks is failed, the entire deal also fails and you don't record anything from the deal.

If the deal is successful, you get to record the deal. Mark all chosen binary tasks as succeeded if not yet already, and record the score (if it's a new high score) for each score task.

The main reason you might want to attempt multiple tasks at once is because some special rule encourages or forces you to do so. However, I also recognize the early campaign is easy and experts might want a challenge already, so attempting multiple tasks at once is offered as an optional rule to spice up your campaign. Perhaps you want to "speedrun" by playing as few deals as possible.

Technically, binary tasks are simply score tasks that read: "score 1 point (i.e. a success) if you don't meet the fail condition". But it's much easier for people to understand binary tasks as their own thing.

Recording your campaign

To record your campaign, I strongly recommend having an off-site method to do so, such as pen and paper or a spreadsheet. You will need to record these items:

  • Which binary tasks you have completed, and what high score you have on each score task
  • Which objectives you have completed
  • Which worlds you have unlocked
  • Any other information you are instructed to record

If your browser has JavaScript enabled, this webpage should record the state of most checkboxes (checked or not) and message boxes (opened or not).

However, JavaScript doesn't seem to be detected on your browser. Try waiting a few seconds, or refreshing the page. In the worst case, the checkboxes and message boxes are functional, the page just won't remember the states when you refresh it.

This webpage should record the state of most checkboxes (checked or not) and message boxes (opened or not). So it can help you record some aspects of the campaign.

However, the records are stored in your browser. Therefore, if you use a different device, the records don't carry over. If you erase all local data of your browser, the records will also be erased. Therefore, consider this to only be a convenience; you are still encouraged to record your progress in another way.

You may reset all campaign data stored by your browser by clicking this button:

Confirm to reset all campaign data? This cannot be undone.

By default, the above rules only allow you to record progress as a group. However, if you have a rotating group and don't expect to play with the same group regularly, you can improvise your own rules for recording progress individually. As someone who plays in rotating groups, this is something that comes to my mind.

As an example, when players play in a group, each player separately records the progress gained from that group. If players with different progression meet, use the lowest progress. A binary task is succeeded only if all players have it succeeded. A score task has a high score equal to the lowest among all players. An envelope is opened, and a world is accessible, only if all players do so.

However, because this is improvisation, you are free to play as you wish. This is just my recommendation.

Player count

Togetrick supports 3p and 4p modes, and the campaign is largely similar in both modes. However, there are occasional differences to allow both modes to have similar difficulty. Most of these differences are in task details, but some worlds may be accessible only to 3p and others only to 4p.

The solo variant follows the 4p rules, with some changes specific to the solo variant.

Some parts of the campaign are not available in solo. (These usually involve a player making a secret choice.)

Switching between 3p and 4p in the campaign is untested, but I imagine it should be possible with minimal disruption to the campaign. Similarly, the solo variant may follow the 3p rules rather than the 4p rules if so desired.

Altrik Matchmaker

In Altrik Matchmaker, your goal is to form as many simultaneous task pairs as possible.

A task pair is a pair of two tasks which you can complete simultaneously.

Forming a task pair: Choose any two unlocked Altrik tasks. Play a deal and succeed on both tasks simultaneously. If you succeed, those two tasks become a task pair.

A task may be involved in any number of task pairs. However, Altrik is primarily concerned about simultaneous task pairs.

Simultaneous task pairs: From the task pairs you have completed, select as many task pairs as you can that do not overlap in any way, i.e. each task is involved in at most one of the selected task pairs. The maximum number of task pairs you can select this way is how many simultaneous task pairs you have at once.

You may retry forming simultaneous task pairs any time you want. Your highest number of simultaneous task pairs is what matters.

It is possible to borrow concepts from graph theory. Each task is a vertex and each task pair is an edge. Then simultaneous task pairs form a matching, and you want to maximize the size of your matching. This game mode came from an earlier iteration of this world, where I put objectives of completing task pairs. I turned it into a game mode instead, thus supporting it more broadly in this world.

You can form task pairs even if they won't help you get more simultaneous task pairs. Those newly-formed task pairs may be helpful in the future; having more options is always good.

There is currently no way to record task pairs on this webpage. Please record them in some other way.

Selvar

In Selvar, push yourself to go far.

Click here if you have unlocked Selvar, so your browser can remember it.

Regular world, with Selvar Loadout rules. Succeed on each of the following tasks. (You may try tasks in any order; they are not necessarily ordered by difficulty.) You may retry tasks freely. However, the process of choosing tasks is vastly different; read the Selvar Loadout rules below.

Special world. Selvar actually resembles a regular world in many ways. It is a collection of tasks, you mark them as completed, and you will never lose completion marks. The crucial difference is that you choose tasks after distributing cards, using the rules of Selvar Loadout.

Tasks in Selvar are to be claimed by an individual player. You look at your hand before you decide what task it's best suited for.

However, multiple tasks will be claimed and active at the same time, forming your loadout. Tasks have varying difficulty, and in order to be allowed to choose a certain task, you must promise your loadout difficulty meets the task's threshold.

Setup: Each player should have a way to indicate tasks they claim, for example with pen and paper.

Selvar Loadout

Tasks in Selvar are individual. A task will be claimed by one player, and they have to succeed on the task. (Of course, other players will likely need to help.) In one deal, multiple tasks will be claimed at once by different players, and these tasks form your group's loadout.

However, you will claim tasks after you look at your hand, and you may claim from all the tasks available to you. This allows you to pick the best task your hand is most suited for.

In addition, each task has a difficulty (under "Diff") and a threshold (under "Thr"). In order to be allowed to claim a certain task, you must promise that your loadout's total difficulty adds up to at least the threshold.

Follow these modified rules for preparing a deal:

  1. Declare a natural number. This is your promised minimum, and you are promising your loadout's total difficulty is at least equal to this promised minimum.
  2. Choose the captain, and distribute cards and pool tokens as normal. You may look at your hands, but do not seed the pool yet.
  3. Starting from the captain and going clockwise, each player has one turn to choose one of the following options:
    • Claim a claimable task. A task is claimable if it has been unlocked, your promised minimum is greater than or equal to the task's threshold, and it has not been claimed by any other player this deal.
    • Pass and do not claim any task.

    4. 3p mode only: If all 3 players claim a task, the captain (and only the captain) gets a second turn to claim an additional task (or pass). If at least one player does not claim a task, the captain does not get this second turn.

  4. Calculate your loadout's total difficulty, by adding up all the difficulties of the tasks you claimed. If this number is less than your promised minimum, the deal is void and immediately fails.
  5. If your loadout's total difficulty meets the promised minimum, you may begin to play. As with usual regular worlds, you only mark the tasks as completed if you succeed on all claimed tasks.

As an example, the "P" group of tasks below all have threshold 3 and difficulty 1. By promising a minimum of 3, these tasks become claimable. Then, starting from the captain and going clockwise, you will have to claim tasks to that your loadout's total difficulty is at least the promised minimum, in this case 3. Because all claimable tasks have difficulty 1, that means at least 3 players must each claim a task.

As usual, you may not discuss once cards have been distributed. This means you may not discuss why you take or don't take a certain task. You are free to make plans before distributing cards though.

Note that you may claim any claimable task, even if you have succeeded on it before. Perhaps your hand doesn't fit any of the remaining incomplete tasks. Doing this will still count its difficulty, so it can help you meet a threshold.

Click to read design notes of this world

This is sort of a marriage between the task selection process in The Crew: Mission Deep Sea and in the LOTR Trick-Taking games. The LOTR Trick-Taking games have the concept of a "long chapter": every deal, each player chooses a character and tries to complete their task, and the goal is to succeed with every character at least once, across however many deals it takes. I borrowed it, and expanded it to cover all the tasks in this world.

I wanted to have tasks of varying difficulty, similar to Deep Sea. However, there was a way to "cheese" the difficulty: take just that one hard task, and everyone else takes easy tasks. To discourage that, I put in task thresholds. The threshold reminded me of Deep Sea's missions, where the difficulty level is simply the sum of task difficulties.

In Deep Sea, it's possible some combinations of tasks are significantly easier or harder than intended. It's also possible to find such synergistic combos here, but because you only need to succeed on a task once, having a combo that can be abused is less of a problem.

The 3p special rule is to allow a 3p group to claim 4 tasks in total, by having the captain claim twice. But if only 3 tasks are claimed, they should still be spread out to all players.

Clarifications for certain tasks

Qa: In 4p, the "exactly two cards" is automatic. You just need them to be trumps.

Qc: For example, if you win three ♣ clubs cards, you may not win exactly three ♦ diamonds cards, nor exactly three ♥ hearts cards, nor all three ♠ spades cards. One of the numbers may be zero.

Qd: The leftover card is not determined until the end of the deal. At the end of the deal, check the leftover card, then check all cards you won from earlier in the deal; you must have the two necessary cards. Once you distribute cards, you may not discuss, including deciding as a group what the leftover card should be. You may plan before distributing cards if it helps, but the cards might not always agree.

Rb: Only cards played from your hand matters for this task; cards played from the pool can be anything. Every card played from your hand must have a number rank (and so cannot be a trump), and its rank must be the lowest (or tied for lowest) among cards in its trick. It doesn't necessarily mean the card must lose the trick.

Rc: For example, if you win five ♣ clubs cards, you must win exactly five ♦ diamonds cards and exactly five ♥ hearts cards. The number of ♠ spades cards you win doesn't matter. You may not simply win no basic suit card at all, you must win at least one.

Rd: Every card you play must either lead its trick or follow suit. This applies to all cards you play, including from your hand and from the pool.

The Wilderness

"The Wilderness" is just a name, it's not an actual world. It's a place for me to put task ideas. Their quality varies. Many of them were tasks that I thought of (sometimes for an older iteration of the game), and haven't been touched up. Some of them seem to be interesting, and are just waiting playtesting to put them to the correct difficulty. Some might be basically ready, but I haven't yet found a place to put them in the campaign.

(To be fair, some tasks in the campaign also haven't been tested, so they may also go back here to The Wilderness as other tasks replace them.)

The texts of these tasks are rushed and might not be templated correctly. They are still just raw ideas after all.

Common tasks

  • The captain wins no tricks. (Originally designed for starting world, very easy.)
  • [No / Every] trick must contain 2+ cards of the same number rank. (Originally from "None or All", can probably find a home somewhere.)
  • No card of rank 9-10 may win a trick. ("None or All", should be relatively easy.)
  • Every card of rank 1 must win a trick. ("None or All". Incredibly hard, requires the 1 holder to be long; it's doable for one, maybe two suits, but three is RNG-heavy.)
  • No number rank may win two tricks. ("None or All". Sounds fun and should find a home somewhere.)
  • Every number rank 1-6 must each win a trick. ("None or All". Also incredibly hard, much like the "all 1s win" above.)
  • No player may win a number of clubs in 1-4. ("None or All". It's a bit of a meme, either someone wins all 9-10 clubs (with one leftover) or two players win 5 each. Probably pretty poorly designed.)
  • [No / Every] player must win #hearts = #diamonds. ("None or All". Probably interesting, but there are some "equal in all suits" tasks too.)
  • [No / Every] trick must be won by the last card of the trick. ("None or All". Also probably interesting. In 3p, at the moment of writing, pool card must be before card in hand, so the last card must be a card in hand, does that affect anything? By the second card of the trick might be better.)
  • Every trick's number ranks must all have the same parity. ("None or All". The "no" version is already Altrik-D. But not sure if I want to keep either.)

Tasks for individual players

  • Win at least 5 tricks. (Originally from "Courage", a starting task for it so it's relatively easy.)
  • Win all 9 other cards of the same suit as the leftover card. (Also "Courage", but sounds much harder.)
  • Every other player must win exactly 1 trick. You must win all the rest. (The Crew 3)
  • Win strictly the most / strictly the least number of tricks. (The Crew 2)

Tasks that look better for a specific player count

(3p) Every player must win at least one ♥ hearts card.
A bit inspired from some The Crew 3 task? At 4p this becomes significantly harder; if everyone follows suit, 10 hearts cards divide cleanly into 3 tricks (the last has discards) but getting to 4 requires some hand management.

Originally part of "None or All". Impossible at 3p.

(4p) Every player must win all 3 cards of some rank.
Originally part of "None or All" (paired with the above). It's significantly easier in 3p.
(4p) Every player must win a number of clubs between 2-3.

Tasks that require helper cards (i.e. the rest of the deck)

  • Shuffle 1 helper card of (3p: each basic suit / 4p: each suit) and deal face-up to each player. They may not win any card of that suit.
  • Shuffle 8 honors and (3p: 7 / 4p: 8) spades cards and deal to each player. (Face-up is easier, face-down is harder.) They must win exactly as many cards as they have honors.
  • Shuffle 10 spades. Deal (3p: 3 / 4p: 2) to each player. Each player chooses one to keep and reveals face-up. They must win (3p: all 3 / 4p: at least 2) cards of the same suit as their card.

Balancing Act

An old scenario where 2+ players each have a running point total. Doing a task gives that player points, and the goal is to get as high total score across all players. But if a player ever has 3+ more points than another, the whole thing ends immediately.

  • +1 point whenever you win a card not in the lead suit.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick you lead.
  • +1 point whenever you win a card whose rank matches the current trick number — i.e. win 1's in 1st trick, win 2's in 2nd trick, etc.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick whose highest and lowest number ranks (ignoring trumps) differ by 4 or less.
  • +1 point whenever you win a card whose rank you have already won (before this trick).
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick using a card whose suit you haven't already won (before this trick).
  • +1 point whenever you win a card of the same suit as your reserve card, as long as your reserve card hasn't been played at the start of the trick.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick containing a card of the same rank as your reserve card, as long as your reserve card hasn't been played at the start of the trick.
  • +1 point whenever you play a non-trump card of the same suit and lower rank as the lead card.
  • +2 points whenever you play a card with equal number rank to another card already played in the trick.
  • +1 point whenever another player (after you) plays a card in a different suit from the card you played this trick.
