About the campaign

Togetrick is presented as a campaign, divided into numerous worlds. Most worlds are regular worlds, which follows some general rules as explaind below. Regular worlds are typically collections of tasks, usually related in theme, and sometimes with a world-wide mechanic. However, special worlds defy categorization; each one is a new surprise. You can find the list of worlds in the table of contents. At the beginning of the campaign, you only have access to one world, Startia.

Most of the content in a world are initially inaccessible, locked behind envelopes represented as white boxes. Do not open these until instructed! Once you are allowed to open an envelope, click/tap it and read its text. Envelopes typically add new tasks to a regular world, but some envelopes introduce major mechanics that change the world quite drastically.

Each world has one or more objectives. Each objective is given in a blue box. When you complete the objective, click/tap the box to reveal a reward, which typically lets you open an envelope or gives you access to new worlds. The campaign has meta-progression, so sometimes a reward lets you open an envelope in another world. Therefore, some advanced objectives in a world may require you to explore other worlds first.

Worlds vary wildly in size. Some worlds are small enough that they can be fully completed in a single game session. Others may require multiple hours to go through. The entire campaign will undoubtedly take a while to play, so you are recommended to keep a record your campaign so you can pause playing and pick it up another time.

Togetrick is presented as a campaign, but I always think of the campaign to be a loose thread to connect a variety of different ways to play using the same basic rules.

If you don't like the campaign, don't let it restrain you. If you don't like the unlock system, feel free to open everything. If you have your own ideas inspired by this campaign, go ahead and play them. In fact, feel free to send me your suggestions; I love to see people iterating on a design, and maybe your ideas might end up being part of the campaign.

Regular world

A regular world is a collection of tasks. Each task only cares about a single deal. This means, when playing in a regular world, each deal is independent; you don't have to record progress inside a task.

There are two kinds of tasks: binary tasks and score tasks.

Binary tasks are yes/no. You either succeed or fail, there is no in-between.

Score tasks award some number of points depending on your performance on the deal. Some score tasks have fail conditions; meeting the fail condition means you fail the task and get no score.

In general, your records — which tasks you have succeeded, what your high scores are — will not be reset. Once you record a success on a binary task, it won't later become failed. Once you record a certain high score, that number won't later be reduced.

Some regular worlds have additional rules applicable only for that world. If they conflict with these general rules, the specific world-wide rules take precedence.

Playing in a regular world

Before distributing cards: If you play in a regular world, first choose one or more tasks to attempt. Usually you choose only one task, but it's legal to choose more.

In order for a deal to be successful, you must succeed on all chosen tasks. (A binary task either succeeds or fails. A score task typically automatically succeeds, but it may fail if there is a fail condition.) If any of the tasks is failed, the entire deal also fails and you don't record anything from the deal.

If the deal is successful, you get to record the deal. Mark all chosen binary tasks as succeeded if not yet already, and record the score (if it's a new high score) for each score task.

The main reason you might want to attempt multiple tasks at once is because some special rule encourages or forces you to do so. However, I also recognize the early campaign is easy and experts might want a challenge already, so attempting multiple tasks at once is offered as an optional rule to spice up your campaign. Perhaps you want to "speedrun" by playing as few deals as possible.

Technically, binary tasks are simply score tasks that read: "score 1 point (i.e. a success) if you don't meet the fail condition". But it's much easier for people to understand binary tasks as their own thing.

Recording your campaign

To record your campaign, I strongly recommend having an off-site method to do so, such as pen and paper or a spreadsheet. You will need to record these items:

  • Which binary tasks you have completed, and what high score you have on each score task
  • Which objectives you have completed
  • Which worlds you have unlocked
  • Any other information you are instructed to record

To help you, this webpage has plenty of checkboxes that you can tick. The ❌ and ✅ symbols indicate checkboxes; you can click/tap on them to toggle their state.

If your browser has JavaScript enabled, this webpage should record the state of most checkboxes (checked or not) and message boxes (opened or not).

However, JavaScript doesn't seem to be detected on your browser. Try waiting a few seconds, or refreshing the page. In the worst case, the checkboxes and message boxes are functional, the page just won't remember the states when you refresh it.

By default, the above rules only allow you to record progress as a group. However, if you have a rotating group and don't expect to play with the same group regularly, you can improvise your own rules for recording progress individually. As someone who plays in rotating groups, this is something that comes to my mind.

As an example, when players play in a group, each player separately records the progress gained from that group. If players with different progression meet, use the lowest progress. A binary task is succeeded only if all players have it succeeded. A score task has a high score equal to the lowest among all players. An envelope is opened, and a world is accessible, only if all players do so.

However, because this is improvisation, you are free to play as you wish. This is just my recommendation.

Player count

Togetrick supports 3p and 4p modes, and the campaign is largely similar in both modes. However, there are occasional differences to allow both modes to have similar difficulty. Most of these differences are in task details, but some worlds may be accessible only to 3p and others only to 4p.

The solo variant follows the 4p rules, with some changes specific to the solo variant.

Some parts of the campaign are not available in solo. (These usually involve a player making a secret choice.)

Switching between 3p and 4p in the campaign is untested, but I imagine it should be possible with minimal disruption to the campaign. Similarly, the solo variant may follow the 3p rules rather than the 4p rules if so desired.

Altrik Matchmaker

Altrik Matchmaker is a special game mode in Altrik. Your goal is to form task pairs, by completing two tasks simultaneously in the same deal. A task may be involved in any number of task pairs at once; however, the primary goal is to have as many simultaneous task pairs as possible, where you select task pairs with no overlaps.

Forming a task pair: Choose any two unlocked Altrik tasks. Play a deal and succeed on both tasks simultaneously. If you succeed, those two tasks become a task pair.

Simultaneous task pairs: From the task pairs you have completed, select as many task pairs as you can that do not overlap in any way, i.e. each task is involved in at most one of the selected task pairs. The selected task pairs form simultaneous task pairs. Many objectives in Altrik want you to have as many simultaneous task pairs as possible. You may retry forming simultaneous task pairs any time you want.

You can form task pairs even if they won't help you get more simultaneous task pairs at the moment. Keep a record of them; they may be helpful to form more simultaneous task pairs in the future.

You may play a deal with more than two tasks. If you succeed, you form every possible task pair involving those tasks.

There is currently no way to record task pairs on this webpage. I'm trying to figure out if there's a good way to do so on this webpage, but in the meantime, please record them yourself some other way.

Click to read Altrik's design notes (contains spoilers)

Altrik was originally designed to be a starting world (after Startia), so it presented itself as just a regular world with a list of tasks. However, this proved to be too open to me, so I thought of a theme on the task. I decided on the theme of "all tricks": each trick has some requirement.

However, I was tempted to introduce some special rules. I wanted the focus to be on the tasks; I should not extensively modify the tasks to fit in the special rules. The simplest mechanism I could think of — which was the basis of The Crew 1 & 2 and a common suggestion in The Crew 3 — was that there are multiple tasks active.

Originally, I simply had objectives of completing multiple tasks simultaneously; doing so once would complete an objective. But I decided to turn it into a game mode instead; this blew open the possibilities and let players try in any way they want.

Altrik Matchmaker can be phrased in terms of graph theory. Each task is a vertex, and each task pair is an edge. Then simultaneous task pairs form a matching, and you want to maximize the size of the matching.

Selvar

In Selvar, push yourself to go far.

Click here if you have unlocked Selvar, so your browser can remember it.

Regular world, with Selvar Loadout rules. Succeed on each of the following tasks. (You may try tasks in any order; they are not necessarily ordered by difficulty.) You may retry tasks freely. However, the process of choosing tasks is vastly different; read the Selvar Loadout rules below.

Selvar Loadout: Click to read the rules

Tasks in Selvar are individual. A task will be claimed by one player, and they have to succeed on the task. (Of course, other players will likely need to help.) In one deal, multiple tasks will be claimed at once by different players, and these tasks form your group's loadout.

However, you will claim tasks after you look at your hand, and you may claim from all the tasks available to you. This allows you to pick the best task your hand is most suited for.

In addition, each task has a difficulty (under "Diff") and a threshold (under "Thr"). In order to be allowed to claim a certain task, you must promise that your loadout's total difficulty adds up to at least the threshold.

Follow these modified rules for preparing a deal:

  1. Declare a natural number. This is your promised minimum, and you are promising your loadout's total difficulty is at least equal to this promised minimum.
  2. Choose the captain, and distribute cards and pool tokens as normal. You may look at your hands, but do not seed the pool yet.
  3. Starting from the captain and going clockwise, each player has one turn to choose one of the following options:
    • Claim a claimable task. A task is claimable if it has been unlocked, your promised minimum is greater than or equal to the task's threshold, and it has not been claimed by any other player this deal.
    • Pass and do not claim any task.

    4. 3p mode only: If all 3 players claim a task, the captain (and only the captain) gets a second turn to claim an additional task (or pass). If at least one player does not claim a task, the captain does not get this second turn.

  4. Calculate your loadout's total difficulty, by adding up all the difficulties of the tasks you claimed. If this number is less than your promised minimum, the deal is void and immediately fails.
  5. If your loadout's total difficulty meets the promised minimum, you may begin to play. As with usual regular worlds, you only mark the tasks as completed if you succeed on all claimed tasks.

As an example, the "P" group of tasks below all have threshold 2 and difficulty 1. By promising a minimum of 2, these tasks become claimable. Then, starting from the captain and going clockwise, you will have to claim tasks to that your loadout's total difficulty is at least the promised minimum, in this case 2. Because all claimable tasks have difficulty 1, that means at least 2 players must each claim a task.

As usual, you may not discuss once cards have been distributed. This means you may not discuss why you take or don't take a certain task. You are free to make plans before distributing cards though.

Note that you may claim any claimable task, even if you have succeeded on it before. Perhaps your hand doesn't fit any of the remaining incomplete tasks. Doing this will still count its difficulty, so it can help you meet a threshold.

Click to read Selvar's design notes

Selvar Loadout is sort of a marriage between the task selection process in The Crew 2 and in the LOTR Trick-Taking games. The LOTR Trick-Taking games have the concept of a "long chapter": every deal, each player chooses a character and tries to complete their task, and the goal is to succeed with every character at least once, across however many deals it takes. I borrowed it, and expanded it to cover all the tasks in this world.

I wanted to have tasks of varying difficulty, similar to The Crew 2. However, there was a way to "cheese" the difficulty: take just that one hard task, and everyone else takes easy tasks. To discourage that, I put in task thresholds. The threshold reminded me of The Crew 2's missions, where the difficulty level is simply the sum of task difficulties.

The 3p special rule is to allow a 3p group to claim 4 tasks in total, by having the captain claim twice. But if only 3 tasks are claimed, they should still be spread out to all players.

Nunik Spectacle: Click to read the rules

A game in Nunik is a spectacle (written "Spec." below) consisting of several deals. Your goal is to perform stunts at the right trick numbers. However, stunts are closely-guarded secrets; you have to explore Nunik's many moons to learn what stunts you may perform.

Playing a game in Nunik: Choose a spectacle. Each spectacle lists a number of targets, which are numbers between 1–8. Some targets are doubled, indicated by the "2×" doubler indicator.

  • You will have to keep track of which targets you have scored. You can use pen and paper for this; start by writing down all targets.
  • Alternatively, you may also use the unused cards of the deck. Take unused ♠ spades cards with ranks matching the targets and lay them down on the table, together with an unused honor card to place across each doubled target.

Each spectacle consists of several deals. In each deal, play a trick normally, but count which trick number you're at: 1st–8th.

If a trick satisfies the conditions of a stunt, you may score the trick:

  • Check if the current trick number is a target. If so, cross it out (or flip the corresponding ♠ spades card face-down).
  • If the target is doubled, instead cross out the "2×" doubler indicator (or remove the honor card). You will need to cross this target once more in a future deal.

A deal may consist of any number of different stunts, but each stunt may only be scored once per deal. It may be scored again in a future deal.

Some edge cases:

  • If a trick satisfies the conditions of a stunt, but the current trick number is not a target, ignore it. The stunt is not scored, which means you may do the stunt again later this deal.
  • If a trick satisfies multiple stunts, the captain chooses which stunt it will be scored as. The other non-chosen stunts are not scored, which means you may do them again later this deal.
  • Scoring a stunt is optional. In the rare case the captain prefers not to score a stunt, they may state so. Then the stunt is not scored, which means you may do the stunt again later this deal. (But it also means you don't get to cross out the associated target.)
  • You must decide whether to score a stunt before you begin the next trick.

You may call off a deal once the trick number has gone past your last uncrossed target, because there's nothing else you do that will affect the spectacle. You may also call off a deal early if you wish to do so.

After completing a deal, check whether you have crossed out all targets (or flipped all the ♠ spades cards face-down). If you do, you have showcased the spectacle, you win! If not, set up another deal if you still have deals remaining. If you run out of deals, you lose; try again.

The conditions to perform stunts are all sealed in Nunik's envelopes. You need to visit Nunik's moons and complete their objectives in order to open these envelopes. Any stunt you learn of may be used in any of your Nunik's games.

Click to read Nunik's design notes

Nunik was actually originally designed as a "hub world". I had several task ideas with very weird rules. However, I didn't think I could support them in a full-fledged "main" world; most of the main worlds have special rules onto them. That led me to the idea of having a hub world leading to "moons", i.e. smaller worlds. Being a smaller world, it felt easier to justify having weird rules and nothing else.

That naturally led me into thinking, what should the moons give back to the hub world? The idea I came up with was, each moon would unlock some sort of task that could be used in the hub world. I wanted players to be encouraged to do multiple moons and unlock multiple tasks, so I decided that a task would "gild" (apply to) a trick, and you needed to gild several tricks at once without repeating tasks. From there, I hashed the details, including broadening the games to cover multiple deals.

Poolone

Poolone's pool is only one card, but it keeps getting refreshed.

Regular world. Succeed on each of the following tasks. (You may try tasks in any order; they are not necessarily ordered by difficulty.) You may retry tasks freely.

This world is a moon of Nunik. If you have unlocked Nunik, you also unlock this world.

Poolone special rules

If you're playing 3 players, first follow these modified rules for preparing the deal:

  1. Determine the captain as normal, and shuffle the deck.
  2. Deal only 1 card to the pool.
  3. The captain gets 10 cards and 2 pool tokens.
  4. Each of the other two players gets 11 cards and 3 pool tokens.

This does mean the captain may only play from the pool twice.

In 4p, there is no change into preparing the deal.

Do not seed the pool. The pool will only have 1 card; a player that plays from the pool has no choice of what card to play.

After each trick: The player who played from the pool must now select a card from their hand and put it into the pool. Thus the pool is replenished after each trick. (After the last trick, the card put into the pool becomes the leftover card in the pool.)

Note that you replenish the pool after the trick. Thus players playing in the trick don't yet know what the next pool card is going to be, but the player replenishing the pool gets to see what cards were played into the pool before replenishing.

In solo, you may choose any card from the corresponding bot's hand to put into the pool, it doesn't have to be the first one.

Cards played from the pool may not be off-suit. (They either lead or follow suit.)
After each trick, the card replenishing the pool must have the same rank as the trick number of the finished trick. (For example, after the 3rd trick, the card replenishing the pool must have rank 3.)
Starting from the second trick, each card played from the pool must have a number rank, and it cannot be the highest or the lowest number rank in its trick (even if tied).
Each trick must be won by the second card played in the trick, but that card must be played from the hand.
Each of two different players must win 5 cards of each non-trump suit.
The captain must win all tricks.

Randaux

In Randaux, tasks have an element of randomness introduced by auxiliary unused cards.

Regular world. Succeed on each of the following tasks. (You may try tasks in any order; they are not necessarily ordered by difficulty.) You may retry tasks freely.

This world is a moon of Nunik. If you have unlocked Nunik, you also unlock this world.

Randaux special rules

All tasks in this world require auxiliary cards, i.e. the unused cards that don't make up the deck. Keep the auxiliary cards separate from the deck. Don't get them mixed up with your hand, the tricks you've won, etc.

The auxiliary cards are dealt "after distributing cards", i.e. after you are dealt your hand, but before you seed the pool. In some cases, you have a decision to do with the auxiliary cards; you may look at your hand before making the decision.

Click here for some specific terminology about auxiliary cards
  • "N unused ♠ spades": Unless otherwise stated, this means 1–N of ♠ Spades. (Recall that 1 is the Ace.) For example, "8 unused ♠ spades" means 1–8♠.
  • "Unused honors": This means J–K of ♣ Clubs, ♦ Diamonds, and ♥ Hearts. ("Honors" by itself means the ranks J–K.) Sometimes this is restricted into e.g. "unused Jacks" or "unused red honors" (i.e. ♦ diamonds and ♥ hearts).
  • "Jokers": Two jokers: one black, one colored. If jokers are not available, you can typically substitute with other unused cards.

Every task in Randaux comes in two variants: the auxiliary cards can be dealt face-up or face-down. Face-up means everyone knows everyone's auxiliary cards. Face-down means only you know yours. The task description tells where you should deal auxiliary cards face-up or face-down.

The face-down variant is obviously more difficult than the face-up variant. Therefore, succeeding on the face-down variant also automatically succeeds the face-up variant. The two variants count as separate tasks for the purpose of counting how many tasks you've succeeded.

When playing solo, you cannot attempt the face-down variant.

Trumphit

In Trumphit, trumps are hitmen that take out their entire tricks.

Regular world. Succeed on each of the following tasks. (You may try tasks in any order; they are not necessarily ordered by difficulty.) You may retry tasks freely.

This world is a moon of Nunik. If you have unlocked Nunik, you also unlock this world.

Trumphit special rules

For all tasks in this world: If a won trick contains a trump, discard the trick instead of its winner keeping it in front of them. The trick and its cards are treated as nonexistent by the game. The cards are not won by any player, the trick is considered to have not been played, etc. There is one exception: the discarded trick's winner will still lead the next trick.

The number of tricks won by all players must be (3p) 1, 2, 3 / (4p) 0, 1, 2, 3 in some order.
Every player must win exactly two ♣ clubs cards.
Every trick must contain exactly one ♣ clubs card.
Every trick must be won by a card with rank 1–5.
No card of rank 6 or 7 may be won by any player. (The leftover card in the pool is not won by any player.)
Everyone except one player must win exactly 0 tricks.

The Wilderness

"The Wilderness" is just a name, it's not an actual world. It's a place for me to put task ideas. Their quality varies. Many of them were tasks that I thought of (sometimes for an older iteration of the game), and haven't been touched up. Some of them seem to be interesting, and are just waiting playtesting to put them to the correct difficulty. Some might be basically ready, but I haven't yet found a place to put them in the campaign.

(To be fair, some tasks in the campaign also haven't been tested, so they may also go back here to The Wilderness as other tasks replace them.)

The texts of these tasks are rushed and might not be templated correctly. They are still just raw ideas after all.

Common tasks

  • The captain wins no tricks. (Originally designed for starting world, very easy.)
  • [No / Every] trick must contain 2+ cards of the same number rank. (Originally from "None or All", can probably find a home somewhere.)
  • No card of rank 9-10 may win a trick. ("None or All", should be relatively easy.)
  • Every card of rank 1 must win a trick. ("None or All". Incredibly hard, requires the 1 holder to be long; it's doable for one, maybe two suits, but three is RNG-heavy.)
  • No number rank may win two tricks. ("None or All". Sounds fun and should find a home somewhere.)
  • Every number rank 1-6 must each win a trick. ("None or All". Also incredibly hard, much like the "all 1s win" above.)
  • No player may win a number of clubs in 1-4. ("None or All". It's a bit of a meme, either someone wins all 9-10 clubs (with one leftover) or two players win 5 each. Probably pretty poorly designed.)
  • [No / Every] player must win #hearts = #diamonds. ("None or All". Probably interesting, but there are some "equal in all suits" tasks too.)
  • [No / Every] trick must be won by the last card of the trick. ("None or All". Also probably interesting. In 3p, at the moment of writing, pool card must be before card in hand, so the last card must be a card in hand, does that affect anything? By the second card of the trick might be better.)
  • Every trick's number ranks must all have the same parity. ("None or All". The "no" version is already Altrik-D. But not sure if I want to keep either.)

Tasks for individual players

  • Win at least 5 tricks. (Originally from "Courage", a starting task for it so it's relatively easy.)
  • Win all 9 other cards of the same suit as the leftover card. (Also "Courage", but sounds much harder.)
  • Every other player must win exactly 1 trick. You must win all the rest. (The Crew 3)
  • Win strictly the most / strictly the least number of tricks. (The Crew 2)

Tasks that look better for a specific player count

(3p) Every player must win at least one ♥ hearts card.
A bit inspired from some The Crew 3 task? At 4p this becomes significantly harder; if everyone follows suit, 10 hearts cards divide cleanly into 3 tricks (the last has discards) but getting to 4 requires some hand management.
(4p) [No / Every] player must win two consecutive tricks.
Originally part of "None or All". The "no" version is easier on 4p than 3p, the "every" version is very precise in 4p and much more loose in 3p.
(4p) No player may win two (or more) cards of the same rank.
Originally part of "None or All". Impossible at 3p.
I've been trying to think of how to make it work. Disregard tricks containing trumps? Consider the pool as an extra player?
(4p) Every player must win all 3 cards of some rank.
Originally part of "None or All" (paired with the above). It's significantly easier in 3p.
(4p) Every player must win a number of clubs between 2-3.
"None or All". The 3p version would say 3-4, but allowing 4 in general makes it significantly easier.

Other old tasks

Every reserve card must win a trick, and [none of them may lead it / each one of them must lead it].
Originally each player had their own one reserve card (not considered for following suit from hand). The first part got turned into Altrik-A ("every trick must be won from the pool").
The "none of them may lead it" part can probably be applied too. The "each one of them must lead it" is impossible. (Originally there's only one reserve card per player, so there's just enough trick in 4p for each player to gain the lead and then play their reserve.)
No trick consists of entirely cards of the same suit as the exiled card.
Every trick must contain a card of the same suit as the exiled card.
Originally the exiled card is determined first. After I toss it into the pool (giving a leftover card), this becomes way harder to track in game.

Balancing Act

An old scenario where 2+ players each have a running point total. Doing a task gives that player points, and the goal is to get as high total score across all players. But if a player ever has 3+ more points than another, the whole thing ends immediately.

  • +1 point whenever you win a card not in the lead suit.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick you lead.
  • +1 point whenever you win a card whose rank matches the current trick number — i.e. win 1's in 1st trick, win 2's in 2nd trick, etc.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick whose highest and lowest number ranks (ignoring trumps) differ by 4 or less.
  • +1 point whenever you win a card whose rank you have already won (before this trick).
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick using a card whose suit you haven't already won (before this trick).
  • +1 point whenever you win a card of the same suit as your reserve card, as long as your reserve card hasn't been played at the start of the trick.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick containing a card of the same rank as your reserve card, as long as your reserve card hasn't been played at the start of the trick.
  • +1 point whenever you play a non-trump card of the same suit and lower rank as the lead card.
  • +2 points whenever you play a card with equal number rank to another card already played in the trick.
  • +1 point whenever another player (after you) plays a card in a different suit from the card you played this trick.
