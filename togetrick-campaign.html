This game is still a work in progress. Nothing on this page or its related pages is set in stone.

About the campaign

Togetrick is presented as a campaign, divided into numerous worlds. Most worlds are regular worlds, which are little more than collections of tasks (usually related in theme). However, special worlds defy categorization; each one is a new surprise. You can read the list of worlds.

At the beginning of the campaign, you only have access to the regular world Startia. Each world has one or more objectives. Each objective heads a gray box. When you complete the objective, you may click/tap the box to reveal its reward. The reward is usually access to a new world; this is how you can explore the worlds in the campaign. In addition, the campaign has meta-progression: progress in a world may affect another world. Thus sometimes it is impossible to complete an advanced objective in a world for the time being, and you need to return after you are more ready.

Worlds vary wildly in size. Some worlds are small enough that they can be fully completed in a single game session. Others may require multiple hours to go through. The entire campaign will undoubtedly take a while to play, so you are recommended to keep a record your campaign so you can pause playing and pick it up another time.

Regular world

A regular world consists of a collection of tasks. Each task only cares about a single deal; there's nothing that requires you to track something across multiple deals. Therefore, when you play in a regular world, each deal is independent.

There are two kinds of tasks: binary tasks and score tasks.

Binary tasks are yes/no. You either succeed or fail, there is no in-between.

Score tasks award some number of points depending on your performance on the deal. Some score tasks have fail conditions; failing the condition results in no score.

Your progress will not get reset. Once you record a success on a binary task, it won't later become failed; once you record a certain high score, that number won't later be reduced.

Some tasks are not immediately accessible to you. Typically, completing objectives in the world — such as succeeding on enough tasks in the world or getting a high enough total high score — will unlock more tasks for you to attempt.

Before distributing cards: If you play in a regular world, first choose one or more tasks to attempt. Usually you choose only one task, but it's legal to choose more (see below).

In order for a deal to be successful, you must succeed on all chosen tasks. (A score task without a fail condition will always count as succeeded.) If any of the tasks is failed, the entire deal also fails and you don't record anything from the deal.

If the deal is successful, you get to record the deal. Mark all chosen binary tasks as succeeded if not yet already, and record the score (if it's a new high score) for each score task.

You may choose any number of tasks to attempt simultaneously. Because you must succeed on every chosen task simultaneously, typically you want to choose only one task at a time.

The major exception is if an objective tells you otherwise. Many advanced objectives require you to succeed on multiple tasks at once.

You may also simply attempt multiple tasks at once just because you like the challenge. If you are trying to "speedrun" the campaign by playing as few deals as possible, this is a good way to tackle more tasks in fewer deals, if you can handle the difficulty.

Tasks may be attempted in any order, you don't have to follow the same sequence as presented.

Binary tasks are simply score tasks with "score 1 point (i.e. a success) if you don't meet the fail condition", but it is much easier for people to understand binary tasks as their own thing.

Recording your campaign

Currently, I don't have a built-in way to let you record your campaign. Use your own method, such as using pen and paper, or a spreadsheet.

You will need to record these items:

  • Which binary tasks you have completed, and what high score you have on each score task
  • Which objectives you have completed
  • Which worlds you have unlocked
  • Any other information you are instructed to record

It's possible to record the progress as individual players rather than as a group. This is ideal if you have a rotating group and don't expect to play with the same group regularly. Each player records any progress they get.

When players with different progress meet, use the lowest progress. For example, a binary task is only considered completed if all players have marked it completed; a score task's high score is the lowest among all players' high scores.

Player count

Togetrick supports 3p and 4p modes, and this is also reflected in the campaign by using the same campaign. However, there are occasional differences to allow both modes to have similar difficulty. Most of these differences are in task details, but some worlds may be accessible only to 3p and others only to 4p.

It's possible to switch modes during the campaign; for instance, if you have a 4-player group, but one is unavailable for a session, you might proceed with the 3p mode for the session. (Note that this is largely untested, and there might be some slight balance issues, but it should largely be fine.)

The solo variant follows the 4p rules, with some changes specific to the solo variant.

Some parts of the campaign are not available in solo. (These usually involve a player making a secret choice.)

Beyond the campaign

Although Togetrick is presented as a campaign, you don't have to use it.

Perhaps you don't like the unlock system, and simply want to try everything one by one. Nothing's stopping you. Play however you want, and enjoy.

Perhaps you have completed the campaign. Congratulations! The campaign is always evolving, and I expect to continue expanding the campaign for the foreseeable future.

Perhaps you want to make use of the basic Togetrick rules, but play with your own creations. Your own tasks and worlds. That's how I came up with Togetrick in the first place, through a desire of playing my own creation. I'm always interested to hear your inventions; let me know and they might just make it to be part of the campaign.

Startia

Everyone has to start somewhere.

Welcome to Togetrick! Startia is the first world of your Togetrick campaign. If you haven't already, read about starting your campaign.

Regular world. Succeed on each of the following tasks. You may retry tasks freely.

A Every player must win at least 1 trick.
B Every card with rank 9 must win its trick.
C The unplayed card in the pool is 6♦ (6 of Diamonds).
Objective: Succeed on all 3 tasks

Congratulations! You have now begun your campaign in earnest. First read about the campaign to learn how the campaign works in general. Once you're ready, head to your newly unlocked world Altrik.

Altrik

In Altrik, all tricks want to shine.

Regular world. Succeed on each of the following tasks. (You may try tasks in any order; they are not necessarily ordered by difficulty.) You may retry tasks freely.

A Every trick must be won by the card played from the pool.
B No trick may be led by a ♣ clubs card.
C Every trick must contain some card whose rank is in the range 1–3.
D Every trick must contain some card with odd number rank and some card with even number rank. (Trumps are not number ranks and have no parity.)
Objective: Succeed on 2 Altrik tasks

You've unlocked world Selvar! You may head there for a new experience.

Additionally, you've unlocked Altrik Matchmaker! It is a special game mode in Altrik.

Altrik Matchmaker

In Altrik Matchmaker, your goal is to form as many task pairs as possible.

Forming a task pair: Choose any two unlocked Altrik tasks. Play a deal and succeed on both tasks simultaneously.

Faithful rule: A task may only be involved in one task pair.

Rearranging task pairs: You may break up a task pair at any time. You may also re-form any task pair you already formed in the past (as long as you obey the faithful rule) without having to replay the deal.

If you happen to have attempted (and succeeded) multiple tasks at the same time while completing this objective, that deal counts toward forming task pairs.

You may form "unofficial" task pairs involving tasks that are already in other task pairs. These unofficial task pairs won't count (because of the faithful rule), but they count as having been formed, so they may help you rearrange later.

You may attempt more than two tasks simultaneously. If successful, you form every possible task pair among the tasks involved in the deal. Most of them will be unofficial, but again, they may help you rearrange later.

Finally, I recommend listing down every task pair you have ever formed, so you don't have to remember them.

In graph theory, task pairs form a matching of tasks.

This game mode came from an earlier iteration of this world, where I put in extra objectives of completing task pairs. I turned it into its own thing.

Objective: Form 1 task pair (complete the previous objective to learn about task pairs)

You've unlocked additional tasks!

E In every trick, the card played from the pool must be the third card played in the trick.
F The 1st trick must contain some card with number rank 1; the 2nd trick must contain a 2; and so on until the 8th trick.
G Every trick must contain at least two different suits.
H In every trick, the second card played must be a ♣ clubs card.

Selvar

In Selvar, push yourself to go far.

Special world. Selvar actually resembles a regular world in many ways. It is a collection of tasks, you mark them as completed, and you will never lose completion marks. The crucial difference is that you choose tasks after distributing cards, using the rules of Selvar loadout.

Every task in Selvar is meant to be claimed by an individual player. You look at your hand before you decide what task it's best suited for.

However, multiple tasks will be claimed and active at the same time, forming your loadout. Tasks have varying difficulty, and in order to be allowed to choose a certain task, you must promise your loadout difficulty meets the task's threshold.

Setup: Each player should have a way to indicate tasks they claim, for example with pen and paper.

Selvar loadout

Task threshold and difficulty: Every task has two numbers attached to it: it's threshold (under column "Thr") and its difficulty (under column "Diff"). In order to be allowed to choose a specific task, your loadout difficulty must meet its task's threshold.

Minimum loadout difficulty: Before distributing cards, you must promise a minimum loadout difficulty, which is a positive integer. You (as a group) have access to all unlocked Selvar tasks whose threshold is at most your promised minimum.

Task claiming: After distributing cards (but before seeding the pool), each player may claim any task you have access to. Your loadout is the set of tasks you claimed, and your loadout difficulty is the sum of difficulties of the claimed tasks. Your loadout difficulty must meet the promised minimum. If not, the deal is immediately void and failed.

As an example, the first group of tasks below all have threshold 3 and difficulty 1. By promising a minimum loadout difficulty of 3, you have access to these tasks. You will then have to claim tasks so that the total difficulty is at least the promised minimum, i.e. 3. Since all of these tasks have difficulty 1, to meet the promise, at least 3 players have to claim a task each.

Follow these modified rules for preparing a deal:

  1. Declare your promised minimum loadout difficulty.
  2. Choose the captain, and distribute cards and pool tokens as normal. You may look at your hands, but do not seed the pool yet.
  3. The captain may claim any task you have access to (i.e. it is an unlocked Selvar task, whose threshold is at most your promised minimum). The captain may instead choose to pass and not claim any task.
  4. In clockwise order, each other player has the same choice of claiming a task or passing. You may not claim a task already claimed by another player.
  5. Compute your loadout difficulty (i.e. sum of difficulties of claimed tasks). If the loadout difficulty is less than the promised minimum, the deal immediately fails.

You cannot discuss once cards have been distributed; this includes discussing what task you want or don't want to take.

Then seed the pool and play out tricks as normal. Check and record progress as with regular worlds: the deal is a success if you succeed on all tasks, and that allows you to mark all tasks in the loadout as succeeded. Note that each task is claimed by a player, so a task's text refers to that player.

In 3p, after each other player gets a choice to claim a task or pass, the captain has one more turn to claim an additional task or pass. This is so that a deal can have 4 claimed tasks, similar to in 4p.

You may choose any task you have access to, regardless of whether it has been completed before. One common reason is that you don't think any of the uncompleted task suits you, so you prefer to play it safe by picking a previously-completed task that you can secure. If the deal succeeds, it lets you mark complete the tasks claimed by other players.

You may have a loadout difficulty that is higher than your promised minimum, if it is useful for you. For that reason, your promised minimum will generally match one of the unlocked thresholds, as there is no incentive to promise any other number (you can just lower your promise without changing any task access).

As you unlock more tasks, it becomes much more time-consuming to pore through the list of all tasks you have access to. You will need to figure out how to handle this problem with your play group.

Clarifications for certain tasks

Qa: In 4p, the "exactly two cards" is automatic. You just need them to be trumps.

Qc: For example, if you win three ♣ clubs cards, you may not win exactly three ♦ diamonds cards, nor exactly three ♥ hearts cards, nor all three ♠ spades cards. One of the numbers may be zero.

Qd: The leftover card is not determined until the end of the deal. At the end of the deal, check the leftover card, then check all cards you won from earlier in the deal; you must have the two necessary cards. Once you distribute cards, you may not discuss, including deciding as a group what the leftover card should be. You may plan before distributing cards if it helps, but the cards might not always agree.

Rb: Only cards played from your hand matters for this task; cards played from the pool can be anything. Every card played from your hand must have a number rank (and so cannot be a trump), and its rank must be the lowest (or tied for lowest) among cards in its trick. It doesn't necessarily mean the card must lose the trick.

Rc: For example, if you win five ♣ clubs cards, you must win exactly five ♦ diamonds cards and exactly five ♥ hearts cards. The number of ♠ spades cards you win doesn't matter. You may not simply win no basic suit card at all, you must win at least one.

Rd: Every card you play must either lead its trick or follow suit. This applies to all cards you play, including from your hand and from the pool.

The Wilderness

"The Wilderness" is just a name, it's not an actual world. It's a place for me to put task ideas. Their quality varies. Many of them were tasks that I thought of (sometimes for an older iteration of the game), and haven't been touched up. Some of them seem to be interesting, and are just waiting playtesting to put them to the correct difficulty. Some might be basically ready, but I haven't yet found a place to put them in the campaign.

(To be fair, some tasks in the campaign also haven't been tested, so they may also go back here to The Wilderness as other tasks replace them.)

The texts of these tasks are rushed and might not be templated correctly. They are still just raw ideas after all.

Common tasks

  • The captain wins no tricks. (Originally designed for starting world, very easy.)
  • [No / Every] trick must contain 2+ cards of the same number rank. (Originally from "None or All", can probably find a home somewhere.)
  • No card of rank 9-10 may win a trick. ("None or All", should be relatively easy.)
  • Every card of rank 1 must win a trick. ("None or All". Incredibly hard, requires the 1 holder to be long; it's doable for one, maybe two suits, but three is RNG-heavy.)
  • No number rank may win two tricks. ("None or All". Sounds fun and should find a home somewhere.)
  • Every number rank 1-6 must each win a trick. ("None or All". Also incredibly hard, much like the "all 1s win" above.)
  • No player may win a number of clubs in 1-4. ("None or All". It's a bit of a meme, either someone wins all 9-10 clubs (with one leftover) or two players win 5 each. Probably pretty poorly designed.)
  • [No / Every] player must win #hearts = #diamonds. ("None or All". Probably interesting, but there are some "equal in all suits" tasks too.)
  • [No / Every] trick must be won by the last card of the trick. ("None or All". Also probably interesting. In 3p, at the moment of writing, pool card must be before card in hand, so the last card must be a card in hand, does that affect anything? By the second card of the trick might be better.)
  • Every trick's number ranks must all have the same parity. ("None or All". The "no" version is already Altrik-D. But not sure if I want to keep either.)

Tasks for individual players

  • Win at least 5 tricks. (Originally from "Courage", a starting task for it so it's relatively easy.)
  • Win all 9 other cards of the same suit as the leftover card. (Also "Courage", but sounds much harder.)
  • Every other player must win exactly 1 trick. You must win all the rest. (The Crew 3)
  • Win strictly the most / strictly the least number of tricks. (The Crew 2)

Tasks that look better for a specific player count

(3p) Every player must win at least one ♥ hearts card.
A bit inspired from some The Crew 3 task? At 4p this becomes significantly harder; if everyone follows suit, 10 hearts cards divide cleanly into 3 tricks (the last has discards) but getting to 4 requires some hand management.
(4p) [No / Every] player must win two consecutive tricks.
Originally part of "None or All". The "no" version is easier on 4p than 3p, the "every" version is very precise in 4p and much more loose in 3p.
(4p) No player may win two (or more) cards of the same rank.
Originally part of "None or All". Impossible at 3p.
I've been trying to think of how to make it work. Disregard tricks containing trumps? Consider the pool as an extra player?
(4p) Every player must win all 3 cards of some rank.
Originally part of "None or All" (paired with the above). It's significantly easier in 3p.
(4p) Every player must win a number of clubs between 2-3.
"None or All". The 3p version would say 3-4, but allowing 4 in general makes it significantly easier.

Tasks that require helper cards (i.e. the rest of the deck)

  • Shuffle 1 helper card of (3p: each basic suit / 4p: each suit) and deal face-up to each player. They may not win any card of that suit.
  • Shuffle 8 honors and (3p: 7 / 4p: 8) spades cards and deal to each player. (Face-up is easier, face-down is harder.) They must win exactly as many cards as they have honors.
  • Shuffle 10 spades. Deal (3p: 3 / 4p: 2) to each player. Each player chooses one to keep and reveals face-up. They must win (3p: all 3 / 4p: at least 2) cards of the same suit as their card.

Balancing Act

An old scenario where 2+ players each have a running point total. Doing a task gives that player points, and the goal is to get as high total score across all players. But if a player ever has 3+ more points than another, the whole thing ends immediately.

  • +1 point whenever you win a card not in the lead suit.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick you lead.
  • +1 point whenever you win a card whose rank matches the current trick number — i.e. win 1's in 1st trick, win 2's in 2nd trick, etc.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick whose highest and lowest number ranks (ignoring trumps) differ by 4 or less.
  • +1 point whenever you win a card whose rank you have already won (before this trick).
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick using a card whose suit you haven't already won (before this trick).
  • +1 point whenever you win a card of the same suit as your reserve card, as long as your reserve card hasn't been played at the start of the trick.
  • +2 points whenever you win a trick containing a card of the same rank as your reserve card, as long as your reserve card hasn't been played at the start of the trick.
  • +1 point whenever you play a non-trump card of the same suit and lower rank as the lead card.
  • +2 points whenever you play a card with equal number rank to another card already played in the trick.
  • +1 point whenever another player (after you) plays a card in a different suit from the card you played this trick.
